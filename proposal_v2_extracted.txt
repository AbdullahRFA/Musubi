TSUNAGU GENERATIONS
Project Proposal | Connecting the Past to the Present | Version 2.0
Project concept: A community exchange platform where people across generations can share knowledge, offer skills and services, request assistance, and build meaningful local connections.
1. Executive Summary
Tsunagu Generations is a responsive, community-based platform designed to connect older and younger generations through knowledge sharing, skill exchange, daily assistance, and companionship. Every registered user can both offer something and request something. Age group is displayed as profile information, not as a role that limits what a person can do.
The platform brings together two simple actions: OFFER (something I can teach or provide) and REQUEST (something I want to learn or receive). Users can publish posts, discover relevant posts and people, respond to requests, communicate, arrange an exchange, and leave feedback after completion.
2. Problem Statement
Older people may have valuable knowledge, practical skills, cultural traditions, and life experiences that have limited opportunities to be shared.
Some community members need help with everyday activities, technology, companionship, or learning new skills.
Younger people may want to learn traditional skills, contribute to their communities, and find meaningful ways to share their time and abilities.
Existing opportunities can be difficult to discover or coordinate at an individual, local, and recurring level.
3. Proposed Solution
Tsunagu Generations provides one shared platform where community members create profiles, publish OFFER or REQUEST posts, search by category, skill, location, and exchange preference, and connect with suitable people. The platform supports free help, skill/service exchange, and paid arrangements. The hackathon payment flow is demonstrative only and does not process real money.
4. Target Users and User Principle
Older Generation (O.G.): community members who may share experience and skills, learn something new, offer services, or request support.
Younger Generation (Y.G.): community members who may share skills and assistance, learn from others, offer services, or request support.
Other community members may also participate where appropriate.
All users follow the same registration process and have the same core capabilities. Age or age group is profile information and does not determine permissions. Every user can create OFFER and REQUEST posts, browse, search, respond, and communicate.

6. Main Features (Functional Requirements)
6.1 Registration and Login
One registration and login process for all users.
Secure email/password authentication.
Create and update a profile with name, profile image, age or age group, location, biography, interests, skills, needs, availability, and contact preferences.
Age group is not a permission or role.
6.2 User Profile and Expertise
Users can list skills, expertise, interests, services they can provide, and services they need.
Users can view public profile information, activity history, completed activity counts, and feedback.
Users can update their own profile.
6.3 Post and Requirement Management
Every registered user can create both OFFER and REQUEST posts.
Posts include title, description, category, skill/service, location, availability, exchange method, optional amount, and status.
Users can edit, delete, close, or mark their own posts as completed.
Quick-post templates reduce typing for common needs and are available to all users, with accessibility-focused design.
6.4 Service Request and Response
Users can respond to posts with an optional message.
Post owners can accept or reject responses.
Users can cancel their own pending requests.
Request status is visible; accepted requests enable the agreed communication workflow.
6.5 Search and Discovery
Search posts and people by keyword, category, skill/expertise, service, user name, and location.
Show matching posts first; if no suitable post is found, show profiles with matching expertise.
Provide filters for category, location, exchange method, availability, and optional age-group filtering.
Community feed supports All, Offers, and Requests filters.
6.6 Chat and Communication
Matched users can use in-app text chat after a request is accepted.
Messages show timestamps and conversation history.
Users can coordinate activity details and meeting times.
Phone contact is optional and shared only with appropriate consent.
6.7 Notifications
Notify users about new requests, request decisions, messages, activity updates, and feedback.
Provide a dedicated notification page.
6.8 Demo Money Transaction
Show the agreed amount and a simulated payment confirmation flow.
Store demo transaction status as Pending, Successful, or Failed.
No real payment gateway or real-money processing is included in the hackathon MVP.
6.9 Feedback and Activity History
Allow feedback and ratings after an activity is marked completed.
Show completed activity counts and reviews on profiles.
Treat activity counts and reviews as reputation signals, not guarantees of safety.
6.10 Homepage and Landing Experience
Use a sticky header with logo, notifications/profile controls, font-size selector (Standard/Large), and language selector (English/Japanese).
Logged-out visitors see a mission illustration banner and Register/Login cards.
Logged-in users see the same banner and homepage structure, but the right-hand hero panel shows My Posts and Pending Requests summary cards.
Show category discovery and a community feed with All, Offers, and Requests filters.
Use responsive desktop/tablet/mobile layouts; authenticated mobile users get bottom navigation.
6.11 About, Support, and Safety
Explain the mission and how the platform works.
Provide help and support information and safety guidance.
Allow users to report inappropriate posts or profiles and block other users.
Do not expose private contact information publicly; share contact details only with consent.

8. Core User Journey
Register or log in and create/update a profile.
Choose to create an OFFER or REQUEST, or discover existing posts.
Create a post using the full form or a quick template.
Search and filter posts; if no suitable post exists, discover matching people.
Respond to a post; the post owner accepts or rejects the request.
After acceptance, communicate, agree on the activity and exchange method, and complete the activity.
Record completion and exchange feedback.
9. Business and Sustainability Perspective
The core value is a trusted local discovery and coordination layer for intergenerational exchange. The platform should demonstrate value to participants and potential community partners such as local organizations, community centres, educational institutions, and local governments. These are potential stakeholder groups, not confirmed partners.
Potential future sustainability options include sponsored community programmes, partnerships with municipalities or community organizations, and clearly disclosed service fees for paid exchanges. These are hypotheses for validation, not part of the hackathon MVP or a confirmed revenue model. The MVP should focus on proving that users can discover, connect, and complete useful exchanges.

12. Expected Outcome and Vision
Tsunagu Generations aims to make it easier for people across generations to exchange knowledge, skills, services, and companionship. It encourages mutual contribution rather than defining one generation only as a helper and the other only as a recipient.
Vision: Connecting the wisdom of the past with the energy of today.
